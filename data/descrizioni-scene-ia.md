# Descrizioni delle scene per il prompt Gemini

Una descrizione in inglese per ogni scena dell'Inferno senza tavola di Doré (elenco in `scene-senza-tavola.md`). Ognuna va incollata al posto di `[descrizione della scena…]` nel prompt di `illustrazioni-ia.md`. Ogni testo dice chi c'è, dove, cosa succede, da dove viene la luce e da che punto si guarda la scena.

**Esclusa:** `inferno/28/scena-2` (Maometto e Alì). La raffigurazione di Maometto è una decisione ancora aperta del proprietario (vedi `scene-senza-tavola.md`), quindi non c'è una descrizione per l'IA.

---

## Canto I

### inferno/1/scena-6 — La profezia del veltro

```
Virgil, calm and solemn, raises one arm and speaks to Dante at the edge of the dark wood. Between them and the open slope, a gaunt, ravenous she-wolf crouches on a rocky path, blocking the way, her ribs showing and her eyes hungry. Far away on the horizon, between low barren hills and a pale sky, a faint vision of a lean greyhound runs, hinting at the savior to come. The only light is the pale dawn glow behind the distant hills, which is the brightest part of the image. We see the scene from behind the two poets, at eye level, looking toward the wolf.
```

## Canto II

### inferno/2/scena-2 — Il dubbio di Dante

```
At the foot of a dark slope at dusk, Dante hesitates, shrinking back with one hand pressed to his chest and his head bowed, full of doubt. Virgil stands facing him and points up at the sky. In a break in the clouds above, two faint visions appear: Aeneas descending alive into the underworld, and Saint Paul being lifted up toward heaven. The last light of day comes from the low left horizon and falls on the clouds and on Dante's troubled face. The viewpoint is slightly low, looking up toward the sky and the visions.
```

### inferno/2/scena-5 — Dante ritrova il coraggio

```
In the foreground, small wildflowers that were bent over by the night frost lift and open their petals as the first sun reaches them. Behind them Dante stands up straight again, his courage restored, while Virgil leads him by the hand along a rough path into the wood. High above, through a break in the clouds, three luminous women appear, blessed figures watching over Dante. The light comes from the dawn sun on the left, with rays crossing the scene and lighting the flowers and Dante's face. We see the scene at ground level so the flowers are large in the foreground.
```

## Canto III

### inferno/3/scena-2 — Gli ignavi

```
Under a sky without stars, a vast dim plain, and across it a long, endless stream of naked, wretched souls runs in a line behind a blank banner that whirls in the air. They are stung by swarms of hornets and wasps, and their faces stream with blood and tears that fall on the ground, where worms gather them. At the edge of the crowd, one hooded figure turns his head away, ashamed. Dante and Virgil stand small at the left, watching. There is no sky light; the only glow comes from a faint pale horizon at the far end of the plain, so the figures near it are the brightest. The viewpoint is a high, wide angle.
```

### inferno/3/scena-5 — Il terremoto e lo svenimento

```
The dark plain on the bank of the river Acheron trembles. A gust of wind rises from the ground and a blinding vermilion flash bursts from the far shore, throwing the shapes of the rocks into black silhouette. In the foreground Dante collapses, falling backward like a man seized by sleep, while Virgil catches and supports him. The flash on the horizon behind them is the only light source, so the two poets are mostly dark against the glow. We see the scene from the side, at eye level, with the bright flash framed between the rocks.
```

## Canto IV

### inferno/4/scena-1 — Il risveglio sull'orlo dell'abisso

```
Dante has just awakened and stands at the very edge of a vast, steep, funnel-shaped abyss, so dark and cloudy that nothing can be seen at the bottom, with a faint sound of sighs rising from it. Virgil beside him is pale and sorrowful, his hand pointing the way down. The view is from a high angle, looking over their shoulders into the immense, sublime void. The light is dim and comes from above and behind, grazing the rim of the cliff and Virgil's face, while the abyss itself is almost solid black.
```

### inferno/4/scena-4 — Il nobile castello e gli spiriti magni

```
On a luminous green hill, in the only lit place of the darkness, the great men and women of antiquity stand, silent, with slow, grave eyes and an air of authority: Hector and Aeneas in armor, Caesar armed with falcon eyes, the warrior women Camilla and Penthesilea, King Latinus with his daughter Lavinia, Brutus, and Lucretia. Apart from the others, alone, stands Saladin. Dante and Virgil stand on the hill looking at them, small and humble. In the background, seven high walls and a stream circle the hill. The light is a calm glow from an unseen source above the hill, lighting the figures against the black surrounding darkness.
```

### inferno/4/scena-5 — I filosofi; la compagnia si divide

```
Aristotle sits in the center, high above the others, in a dignified pose, surrounded by the great philosophers and scientists of antiquity who stand and look up to him with admiration. Socrates and Plato stand closest to him, and Democritus, Diogenes, Heraclitus, Euclid, Ptolemy, Hippocrates and Avicenna can be recognized by scrolls, books, an astrolabe and a flask. Dante and Virgil stand at the edge of the group; in the background the road leads out, down toward darkness. The light is a soft, steady glow on the philosophers' hill, brightest on Aristotle, with deep black shadows behind. The viewpoint is slightly low, looking up at the seated master.
```

## Canto VI

### inferno/6/scena-1 — La pioggia eterna

```
In the third circle, a cold, heavy, never-ending storm of hail, dirty water and snow pours from a black sky onto a foul, stinking mud. Naked souls lie half sunk in the slush, their bodies bent against the downpour. In the foreground Dante, just coming back to his senses, is held up by Virgil. The only light is a weak grey glow behind the curtains of rain, making the falling streaks of hail the brightest part of the image. We see the scene from slightly above, with the rain slanting across the whole frame.
```

### inferno/6/scena-4 — La profezia su Firenze

```
Ciacco, a gaunt, pale soul, sits up in the mud and points a bony hand toward Dante, who bends toward him, while Virgil stands behind with his arms crossed. In the smoky, rain-filled sky above, a ghostly vision of Florence appears: a city of tall towers on a hill, with two armed factions clashing in its streets under white and black banners, one side overcoming the other. The only light comes from the vision in the clouds, which lights the faces of the three figures from above. We see the scene at eye level, with the vision filling the upper half.
```

### inferno/6/scena-5 — Il giudizio universale e Pluto

```
Virgil raises one hand toward the sky as he explains the Last Judgement. In the stormy clouds above, a vision appears: a great angel blowing a trumpet while the dead rise from their tombs and take back their bodies, their arms raised. At the bottom of the image, the gluttons lie in the mud under the cold rain, and Dante listens, with the poets turned toward a dark, steep descent at the edge of the circle where they are heading. The light comes from the vision in the sky, a ray descending from the trumpet angel, which is the brightest part of the image. We see the scene from slightly below, looking up toward the vision.
```

## Canto VII

### inferno/7/scena-3 — La Fortuna

```
Virgil, calm, explains with an open hand, while Dante listens at his side. Above them, in a vast sky, towers a huge serene winged goddess, an angelic intelligence, wearing a calm expression and turning a great sphere in her hands, passing crowns, scepters and treasures from one hand to the other and from one people to the next. Far below, small, blind human figures reach up and fight over the gifts that fall. The light radiates from the figure of Fortune, making her the brightest part of the image, and lights Virgil's and Dante's faces from above. We see the scene from a low angle, looking up at the goddess.
```

## Canto VIII

### inferno/8/scena-3 — La città di Dite

```
Across a black, stagnant marsh, the boat of Phlegyas carries the two poets toward a huge city of walls that seem to be made of iron, with high towers and mosques whose red outline glows as if heated by a fire inside. Phlegyas, a wild, muscular old man, rows at the stern; Dante and Virgil sit still, looking ahead. The only light is the red glow from the city itself, reflected on the dark water, so the walls and towers are the brightest part of the image. We see the scene from low on the water, with the city rising ahead.
```

## Canto IX

### inferno/9/scena-1 — L'attesa davanti a Dite

```
On the bank before the dark walls of Dite, Virgil stands wrapped tightly in his cloak, his head tilted as if listening, uneasy, waiting for help that does not come. Dante shrinks behind him, looking up with fear. A thick, pale fog hides the foot of the walls. In the swirling fog at the left, a faint ghostly vision appears: the sorceress Erichtho calling back a spirit to send it into the deep. The only light is a pale glow in the fog behind the walls, so the fog and Virgil's profile are the brightest part of the image. We see the scene at eye level from the side.
```

## Canto X

### inferno/10/scena-1 — Gli epicurei

```
A narrow path runs between the dark wall of the city and a long row of open stone tombs, with their heavy lids raised. A red glow burns inside the tombs, and a faint smoke rises. Dante bends over the edge of one tomb to look inside, while Virgil stands behind him, one hand lifted in explanation. The light comes only from the fire inside the tombs, lighting the stone rims and the poets from below, and each tomb in the distance is a small glowing point. We see the scene from the path with a strong receding perspective along the row of tombs.
```

### inferno/10/scena-3 — Cavalcante

```
From an open tomb in the foreground, an old man rises as far as his chin, kneeling in the grave and looking around desperately, his hands raised, searching for his son beside Dante. Dante, standing at the edge, bends toward him in surprise. Behind them, in another burning tomb, Farinata stands upright with his chest out, disdainful and indifferent. The light is the red glow of the fire in the tombs, lighting the old man's pleading face from below and leaving the background dark. We see the scene at eye level, close, focused on the old man.
```

### inferno/10/scena-4 — La profezia dell'esilio

```
Farinata, a huge, proud figure, rises from the waist out of a burning tomb, his chest and head lifted, looking at Dante with great scorn, as though he despised Hell itself. Dante stands before him, close, serious and tense; Virgil pushes him gently forward. In the background, other open tombs glow, and in two of them one can see an emperor with a crown, Frederick II, and a cardinal in a hat. The light is the red fire in the tombs, brightest on Farinata's face and chest. We see the scene from a slightly low angle, so that Farinata towers over Dante.
```

### inferno/10/scena-5 — Virgilio conforta Dante

```
Dante, troubled by the prophecy of exile, walks away from the wall beside Virgil, who puts a hand on his shoulder to comfort him. High in the dark sky above them, a faint vision appears: Beatrice, a veiled woman with luminous eyes, looking down gently. The path leads toward a valley in the distance, from which a dark, foul vapor rises. The light comes from the vision of Beatrice, which is the brightest part of the image and lights the poets from above. We see the scene from behind, following the two poets as they walk away.
```

## Canto XI

### inferno/11/scena-2 — Violenti e fraudolenti

```
On the edge of a broken, steep cliff at the top of the lower Hell, Virgil sits and points down with one hand, explaining, while Dante sits next to him and listens. Beyond the edge, an immense stepped gulf descends in concentric rings: a river of blood, a dark wood, a plain of burning sand, then rows of stone ledges, and at the bottom a pale glimmer of ice. The scale is huge, with the poets tiny. The light comes from the faint glow of the burning sand far below, rising along the cliff face. We see the scene from behind the poets, at a high angle.
```

### inferno/11/scena-3 — Incontinenza e usura

```
Dante and Virgil stand up from the edge of the cliff, Virgil holding an open book (Aristotle's Ethics) under one arm and pointing at the sky with the other hand. In the east, dawn is breaking: the constellation of the Fishes glimmers on the horizon, while the Wain (the Great Bear) lies low in the northwest. Behind them, the steep path down begins among broken rocks. The only light is the first pale glow of dawn on the horizon, which is the brightest part of the image. We see the scene from slightly below, with the sky large.
```

## Canto XII

### inferno/12/scena-2 — La frana del terremoto

```
A huge ruined slope of broken boulders, cracked and heaped up where the rock collapsed in the great earthquake. Virgil, in the foreground, points to the fallen rocks and explains; Dante follows, carefully stepping over the stones. Far below, at the bottom of the slope, a river of boiling blood steams in the dark. The light is the red glow of the boiling river, rising from below and lighting the undersides of the boulders. We see the scene from above and behind the poets, looking down the slope.
```

### inferno/12/scena-5 — I tiranni e gli omicidi nel sangue

```
A wide river of boiling blood, filled with steam. Sinners are sunk in it to different depths: tyrants wearing crowns up to their eyebrows, murderers up to the throat, and, farther on, others only to the waist. Along the bank walks the centaur Nesso, holding a bow, pointing out the damned to Dante and Virgil. In the background, at the right, other centaurs gallop along the shore, shooting arrows at anyone who tries to come out of the blood. The light is the red glow of the river, lighting the wet faces and the steam. We see the scene from the bank, at eye level, with the river receding into the distance.
```

## Canto XIII

### inferno/13/scena-3 — Pier delle Vigne

```
Dante stands with a broken twig in his hand, shocked, his other hand stretched in compassion toward a black, twisted, thorny tree whose trunk bleeds dark blood from the wound and in whose bark a sorrowful human face seems to appear. The tree speaks, and Virgil stands behind Dante, watching. Harpies with human faces and feathered bellies perch in the branches overhead. The light is dim and comes from the left through the trees, lighting the wounded tree and Dante's face. We see the scene at eye level, close, with the tree in the foreground.
```

### inferno/13/scena-4 — Il destino dei suicidi

```
A dense wood of black, leafless, twisted, thorny trees, each one a human soul transformed. Harpies perch in the branches, tearing at the leaves, and the trees weep blood from their wounds. In the foreground, Pier's tree speaks while Virgil questions it and Dante stands aside, overcome. In the far depth, between the trunks, a vision of the future: human bodies hanging from the thorns of their own trees. The light is dim and comes from the right through the trunks, lighting the bark and the blood. We see the scene at eye level with the trunks receding into darkness.
```

## Canto XIV

### inferno/14/scena-1 — Il sabbione

```
At the edge of the dark forest, a vast, bleak desert of pale sand opens, stretching to the horizon with no plants. Flakes of fire fall slowly from the sky like snow in the mountains. Groups of naked souls fill the plain: some lie flat on their backs, some sit hunched up and holding their knees, others walk without rest. Dante and Virgil stand on the edge of the wood, tiny against the vast plain. The light comes from the falling flames, which are the brightest part of the image, with the sand lit from above. We see the scene from a high, wide angle.
```

### inferno/14/scena-3 — Capaneo

```
A huge dannato lies on the burning sand, defiant, as if the rain of fire did not touch him, one arm raised, his face twisted in scorn toward a stormy sky with lightning. This is Capaneus, who still challenges Jupiter to strike him. Virgil, standing before him, shouts at him with a powerful gesture, his arm extended, while Dante stays behind, uneasy. Flakes of fire fall around them. The light comes from the lightning and the falling flames, lighting Capaneus' muscular body from above. We see the scene from a low angle, looking up at the giant.
```

### inferno/14/scena-4 — Il ruscello rosso e il Veglio di Creta

```
Inside a mountain cave on the island of Crete, a gigantic old man stands, his back turned to Damietta and his face to Rome. His head is gold, his arms and chest silver, his body down to the groin bronze, and his legs iron, with the right foot of clay. A fissure runs through every part of him except the gold head, and tears pour from it, streaming down through the rock into the abyss. At the foot of the statue, in the bottom corner, Virgil points up to explain to Dante, and a small red stream flows out of the rock. The light comes from the mouth of the cave behind, lighting the old man's metal body from above. We see the scene from a low angle, which emphasizes the colossal scale.
```

## Canto XV

### inferno/15/scena-1 — Sull'argine

```
The two poets walk along one of the stone embankments above a steaming brook, protected from the burning flakes by the vapor, like the dikes the Flemish and the Paduans build against the water. On the sand beside the embankment, a line of souls looks up at them, squinting their eyes as an old tailor squints to thread his needle. The light is dim, as under a new moon, and comes from the flakes of fire in the sky and the pale vapor, lighting the backs of the poets. We see the scene from the embankment, with a strong perspective along the dike.
```

### inferno/15/scena-3 — La profezia di Brunetto

```
Brunetto Latini, an old man with a burned, creased face, walks on the sand at the foot of the embankment and looks up at Dante, one finger raised as if passing on a prophecy. Dante bows his head toward him with great affection, almost reaching down to touch him. In the far background, a hill town glimmers: Fiesole above Florence, with towers and a ring of walls. The light comes from the falling flames and the pale vapor of the brook, lighting the two faces. We see the scene from the side, at eye level, close.
```

### inferno/15/scena-4 — I compagni di Brunetto

```
In the far distance, a small running figure, Brunetto, races away along the burning sand, like a winner of the green cloth race at Verona. In the foreground, Dante watches him leave from the embankment. Behind Dante, a group of scholarly men on the sand: a bishop wearing a mitre, a grammarian carrying a book, and a doctor of law in a long robe, all marked by the same sin. A new troop of souls approaches from the left. The light comes from the falling flames and the vapor, lighting Dante and the foreground figures. We see the scene from a high angle looking down the embankment.
```

## Canto XVI

### inferno/16/scena-1 — Le tre ombre

```
Three naked, burned figures break away from a crowd and run toward the embankment, wheeling around one another like wrestlers before the fight, their faces turned toward Dante while their feet go the opposite way. Dante bends over the edge, drawn toward them, and Virgil stands beside him, watching. In the far distance, a faint roar and mist of a great waterfall. The light comes from the falling flames and the glow of the sand, lighting the three bodies from above. We see the scene from the embankment, looking down at the figures.
```

### inferno/16/scena-2 — Firenze, la gente nuova e i subiti guadagni

```
Dante stands on the embankment with his arms outstretched toward the horizon, crying out against his city. Below him, the three burned shades raise their hands up toward him, pleading to be remembered in the world. In the distance, under a stormy sky, a skyline of towers glimmers: Florence. The light comes from the storm clouds far behind the city, silhouetting the towers, with the flakes of fire lighting Dante's face. We see the scene from slightly below, with Dante large and the city small on the horizon.
```

### inferno/16/scena-3 — La cascata e la corda

```
A great waterfall of the river Phlegethon drops from a sheer cliff into the abyss with a deafening roar and clouds of mist. In the foreground Virgil has taken from Dante's waist the cord he wore as a belt and flings it far out into the void, while Dante watches with his hands still at his waist. Below, in the black depth, something rises through the air, swimming: the shadowy shape of a monster with wide wings coming up from the abyss. The light comes from the pale mist of the waterfall, which is the brightest part of the image. We see the scene from the cliff edge, at a high angle looking down.
```

## Canto XVII

### inferno/17/scena-2 — Gli usurai

```
Dante, alone, stands at the edge of the burning sand where a row of usurers sit hunched, trying to protect themselves from the fire and the hot ground with their hands, like dogs in summer shaking off fleas. Each one has a purse hung at his neck, with a coat of arms painted on it: a blue lion on yellow, a white goose on red, a blue sow on white. One of them sticks his tongue out like an ox licking its nose. In the far background, the winged monster Geryon waits with Virgil on its back. The light comes from the falling flakes of fire, lighting the crouched bodies from above. We see the scene at eye level from behind Dante.
```

## Canto XVIII

### inferno/18/scena-1 — Malebolge

```
A vast bowl of stone the color of iron, descending in ten concentric trenches toward a deep central well, crossed by rock bridges like the spokes of a wheel, like the drawbridges of a castle. At the top of the cliff in the foreground, Dante and Virgil have just climbed down from Geryon, who flies away into the dark at the left. The poets are tiny against the immense, geometric space. The light comes from a dim glow high above, grazing the tops of the ridges so the ridges are lighter and the trenches are in deep black shadow. We see the scene from a very high angle, looking down into the whole of Malebolge.
```

### inferno/18/scena-3 — Giasone

```
On a stone bridge above the first trench, Virgil points down toward a tall, noble figure walking apart from the others: Jason, who still has a regal bearing, head high, not weeping despite the pain. Along the trench, naked seducers walk in rows, whipped by horned demons with great scourges. In the background, a faint vision in the dark: the ship Argo on a stormy sea. Dante stands next to Virgil, looking down. The light comes from a dim glow high above the bridge, lighting Jason's head and shoulders. We see the scene from the bridge, at a high angle.
```

## Canto XIX

### inferno/19/scena-1 — I fori infuocati dei simoniaci

```
A sloping wall of rock, its whole surface pierced by round holes in rows, like baptismal fonts. From each hole, a sinner is stuck head down, and only his legs stick out, kicking, with the soles of his feet on fire. Dante and Virgil stand on the rim of the trench at the top, looking down at the holes, Dante with a hand on his chest in awe. The light comes from the flames on the soles of the feet, a rhythm of small bright points on the dark rock. We see the scene from a high angle, with the rows of holes receding along the slope.
```

### inferno/19/scena-3 — L'invettiva contro i papi simoniaci

```
Virgil carries Dante in his arms up the rocky crag, away from the trench, his face pleased. Behind them, in the hole, a pair of legs kicks furiously, the feet in flames: Pope Nicholas III. In the stormy sky above, a vision from the Apocalypse: a huge woman wearing a papal tiara sits astride a monstrous beast with seven heads and ten horns, a symbol of the corrupted Church. The light comes from the flames in the trench, below, and from a faint glow behind the vision in the sky. We see the scene from the side, with a rising diagonal composition.
```

## Canto XX

### inferno/20/scena-1 — Gli indovini con la testa voltata

```
In a circular valley, a silent procession of naked souls walks slowly and weeping, their heads twisted backwards on their necks so that they walk backward, and their tears run down their backs. Dante leans against a rock at the side, weeping at the sight of the distorted human forms, while Virgil stands before him, stern, scolding him for his pity. The light is the dim, pale glow of a moon, lighting the procession from above and the side. We see the scene from a slightly high angle, with the procession curving into the distance.
```

### inferno/20/scena-2 — Anfiarao, Tiresia, Aronta

```
Virgil points out three soothsayers of antiquity in the procession: Amphiaraus, in armor, with his face turned backwards, the one the earth swallowed in front of Thebes; Tiresias, holding a rod with two entwined serpents, his body showing the change from man to woman; and Aruns, with his back against the belly of the next sinner. Behind Aruns, a faint vision of a cave among white marble cliffs, with the stars and the sea in view. Dante watches from the side. The light is the pale glow of the moon, lighting the three figures from above. We see the scene at eye level.
```

### inferno/20/scena-3 — Manto e l'origine di Mantova

```
A naked woman with long loose hair covering her chest walks backward with her head twisted behind her: Manto, the sorceress. Virgil, beside Dante, tells the story of his birthplace. In the background, a wide, still marsh where the river Mincio spreads, and on a small dry island at its center the ruins of a bare town founded on her bones, with a wall and a few towers. The light is the pale glow of the moon, reflected on the marsh, lighting Manto's hair and shoulders. We see the scene from the side, at eye level, with the marsh stretching behind.
```

### inferno/20/scena-4 — Euripilo, Michele Scotto e gli altri indovini

```
Virgil points out the last soothsayers of the procession: a lean magician in a long robe (Michael Scot), an astrologer holding an astrolabe, a shoemaker holding his last, and a group of wretched women who left needle, spindle and loom for spells, holding herbs and wax figures. Dante stands behind, watching. On the horizon, the moon sets over the sea, huge and low, signaling dawn. The light comes from the setting moon, which is the brightest part of the image, silhouetting the figures in profile. We see the scene from the side, at eye level.
```

## Canto XXI

### inferno/21/scena-1 — La pece bollente

```
From a bridge, the poets look down into the fifth trench, a strangely dark lake of thick black pitch, boiling and swelling in slow, heavy bubbles that rise and burst, like the pitch that is boiled in winter in the Venice shipyard to mend ships. Dante leans on the parapet, staring in fright, while Virgil stands at his side. The steep stone walls of the trench rise on both sides. The light comes from a pale glow in the sky above, with only a faint shine on the surface of the pitch. We see the scene from the bridge, at a high angle, looking down.
```

### inferno/21/scena-4 — La scorta dei dieci diavoli

```
Ten grotesque devils, with horns, bat wings, hooked pitchforks and grinning faces, line up before the poets: Malacoda, their chief, with the largest wings, gestures as he gives his orders, while Barbariccia sticks out his tongue in mockery. Dante shrinks against Virgil, holding his arm, afraid, and Virgil holds him calmly. The ground is rocky and the pitch glows behind them. The light is a red glow from below and behind, from the pitch lake, lighting the devils from beneath. We see the scene at eye level, from the poets' side, with the devils filling the frame.
```

## Canto XXII

### inferno/22/scena-1 — In cammino con i diavoli

```
Along the stone rim of the lake of pitch, the ten devils march in a column with their hooks on their shoulders, and Dante and Virgil walk among them. In the pitch, sinners surface to ease their pain, arching their backs like dolphins, while others keep only their snouts out, like frogs at the edge of a pond. As Barbariccia approaches, they all dive back in at once. The light is a dull red glow from the pitch, lighting the devils' horns and the wet backs of the sinners. We see the scene from slightly above, along the rim.
```

### inferno/22/scena-2 — Il barattiere navarrese

```
A demon hooks a sinner by his sticky hair and pulls him out of the pitch like an otter, the black tar dripping from his body, while he cries out. Other devils crowd around with their hooks ready to flay him, among them Barbariccia and Rubicante, snarling. Dante and Virgil watch from the side, Virgil leaning in to question the wretch. The light is a red glow from the pitch, lighting the dripping body and the demons' faces from below. We see the scene at eye level, close to the action.
```

## Canto XXIII

### inferno/23/scena-1 — La paura dell'inseguimento

```
The two poets walk in silence, one behind the other, like friars on the road. Dante turns his head to look back, afraid. Far behind, in the dark air above the rocks, a pack of black devil shapes with hooks has begun to pursue them, wings beating. Virgil, in front, faces forward, listening, his hand reaching back to Dante. The light is a red flare from the pitch behind, outlining the devils' wings and the two poets in silhouette. We see the scene from the side at a low angle, with the pursuers in the distance.
```

## Canto XXIV

### inferno/24/scena-1 — Lo sgomento e il conforto

```
Virgil turns back toward Dante with a sweet, tender look, one hand extended to lift his chin, while Dante, troubled, bows his head. Behind them, a collapsed stone bridge and the dark trenches. In the pale light of dawn at the right, the frost on the rocks glimmers like snow, as when a peasant sees the white frost and despairs, then takes heart again. The light is the cold dawn at the right, brightest on Virgil's gentle face. We see the scene at eye level, close, with the two faces in the center.
```

### inferno/24/scena-2 — La faticosa salita

```
A huge slope of broken rocks left by a landslide. Virgil, above, studies the next boulder and reaches a hand down to help Dante, who climbs on all fours, out of breath and struggling, from stone to stone. At the very top of the slope, the ridge of the ruined bridge cuts the sky. The scale is vast, with the poets small against the rocks. The light comes from the right, grazing the edges of the boulders and lighting Virgil's outstretched arm. We see the scene from below, looking up the slope.
```

### inferno/24/scena-4 — Vanni Fucci, incenerito e risorto

```
At the edge of a pit filled with serpents where naked thieves run in terror, a serpent leaps and bites a sinner on the back of the neck. In an instant, he bursts into flame and collapses into a heap of ashes, which then gather and rise again into the same body, like the phoenix, and he sits dazed, like a man who has fallen in a fit. Dante and Virgil watch from the rocky bank above. The light is the flash of the flames at the moment of burning, which is the brightest part of the image. We see the scene from the bank, at a high angle.
```

## Canto XXV

### inferno/25/scena-1 — Le fiche di Vanni Fucci e Caco

```
Vanni Fucci stands upright, his arms raised toward the sky, both hands making the obscene fig sign against God, while two serpents coil around his neck and arms to bind him. Dante recoils at the left. At the right, running along the bank, comes the centaur Cacus, his horse's back covered with snakes, and a fire-breathing dragon on his shoulders. The light is the flame from the dragon's mouth, lighting Cacus and Vanni Fucci from the right. We see the scene at eye level, with Vanni Fucci in the center.
```

### inferno/25/scena-3 — Lo scambio tra Buoso e Francesco

```
In the center, a man and a small black serpent face each other, locked together, their mouths and the wound in the man's navel streaming smoke that meets in the air. The two bodies are changing their natures: the man's legs fuse into a tail, while the serpent's tail splits into legs; the man's arms shorten and the serpent's grow, their faces merging and separating. Dante stands at the side, astonished, with Virgil. Another sinner stands apart, unchanged, and in the dark the rest of the trench is full of snakes. The light is a faint glow coming from the smoke itself, lighting the metamorphosing bodies. We see the scene at eye level, close.
```

## Canto XXVI

### inferno/26/scena-1 — L'invettiva contro Firenze

```
High above the trench of the thieves, the two poets climb the rough stone steps of the ridge, using their hands as well as their feet. In the dark sky behind them, a ghostly vision of Florence: a huge winged woman, crowned with towers, spreading her wings over land and sea. Below, in the trench, the dim shapes of naked thieves among serpents. The light comes from a pale glow behind the winged vision, lighting the poets' backs. We see the scene from below and behind, looking up at the climbing poets.
```

### inferno/26/scena-2 — Le fiamme dell'ottava bolgia

```
From the top of a stone bridge, Dante leans over so far that he would fall without a rock to hold on to, looking down into the eighth trench, a dark valley full of thousands of flickering flames, like fireflies seen by a peasant on a hillside on a summer evening. Each flame hides a sinner. Virgil stands beside him, holding him. In the sky, far above, a small, faint vision of the chariot of fire carrying the prophet Elijah to heaven. The light comes from the countless small flames in the valley, making a field of bright points against the black. We see the scene from the bridge, at a high angle.
```

### inferno/26/scena-4 — L'ultimo viaggio di Ulisse

```
A small, old wooden ship sails on a vast, dark ocean, with a few weary sailors rowing with their oars like wings, past the Pillars of Hercules that stand at the left. Far ahead on the horizon rises an enormous conical mountain, wrapped in clouds, the highest ever seen. From the new land, a whirlwind is rising, about to strike the ship. The sky is full of unknown southern stars. The light is the pale moon low on the right, reflecting on the swell, lighting the sails and the faces of the men. We see the scene from the sea, at a low angle, with the mountain in the distance.
```

## Canto XXVII

### inferno/27/scena-1 — Una nuova fiamma chiede della Romagna

```
On the ridge of a stone bridge, a tall flame stands, its tip trembling and swaying like a tongue, from which confused, muffled sounds come, like the groans of a bull of brass. Dante bends toward it, listening intently, with Virgil at his side. In the far distance behind them, the flame of Ulysses departs and fades into the dark. The light is the flame itself, which is the brightest part of the image, lighting Dante's and Virgil's faces from the front. We see the scene at eye level, with the flame in the center.
```

### inferno/27/scena-2 — La Romagna nel 1300

```
Dante, speaking up toward the flame with one hand raised, answers the spirit. In the sky above him, a wide vision of Romagna: a plain of walled cities with towers, each marked by a heraldic banner: an eagle over Ravenna, a green-clawed lion over Forlì, two mastiffs tearing a banner over Rimini, a small lion on a white field over Faenza and Imola. Tyrants on horseback ride through each town. The light comes from the flame at the left, lighting Dante's face, and a faint glow behind the vision. We see the scene at eye level, with the vision filling the upper half.
```

### inferno/27/scena-3 — Il consiglio fraudolento

```
In a stone room of a papal palace, Pope Boniface VIII sits on a throne wearing the tiara, cunning, leaning forward to hear a Franciscan friar in a cord-belted habit, who kneels and whispers advice in his ear. Through a window at the back, one can see the besieged fortress of Palestrina on a hill. The light comes from the high window at the back, lighting the pope's face and the friar's hood, with the rest of the room in deep shadow. We see the scene at eye level, as a vision seen from the front.
```

### inferno/27/scena-4 — San Francesco e il diavolo loico

```
In a dim cell, a dying friar lies on a bed. Saint Francis, in a simple habit with the stigmata on his hands, has arrived to take his soul, but a dark, sharp-featured devil with clawed hands seizes the soul, a small naked figure, by the hair and drags it away, arguing triumphantly with a finger raised like a logician. The saint looks on, helpless. The light is a ray coming from behind Saint Francis, lighting the devil and the soul. We see the scene at eye level, close to the three figures.
```

## Canto XXVIII

### inferno/28/scena-1 — La bolgia del sangue

```
The floor of the ninth trench is covered with a field of mutilated bodies: severed limbs, split skulls, open chests, bodies cut in half, all among pools of blood. In the clouds above, fleeting visions of old battles, soldiers and horses fighting from the time of the Trojans to the field of Tagliacozzo, as if all the wounded of the wars of Italy were here. Dante and Virgil stand at the edge, appalled. The light is dim and comes from above and the left, lighting the white bodies against the black ground. We see the scene from a high angle looking down into the trench.
```

### inferno/28/scena-4 — Mosca dei Lamberti

```
A tall, haggard man walks in the dark, holding up his arms, both hands cut off, the bloody stumps raised against the black air, his face smeared with blood, bent in anguish. Dante stands in front of him, speaking, and Virgil behind. In the background, other mutilated shapes drift in the gloom. The light is faint and comes from the left, lighting the raised stumps and the sinner's face. We see the scene at eye level, close.
```

## Canto XXX

### inferno/30/scena-2 — Maestro Adamo, il falsario di monete

```
A sinner lies on the ground, his belly enormously swollen with dropsy, in the shape of a lute, while his wasted face and tiny head are thrown back, his lips parted by thirst like a man with fever. This is Master Adamo, the coin forger. Dante and Virgil stand beside him. In a vision above him, green hills and streams of the Casentino run cool toward the Arno, torturing him with the memory of water. The light is a faint glow, lighting his swollen belly, and the vision above is brighter, a pale, cool shimmer. We see the scene at eye level, close.
```

### inferno/30/scena-3 — La rissa tra maestro Adamo e Sinone

```
Two sinners fight on the ground in a stinking steam. Sinon, the Greek, lying on his face with fever, strikes the drum-tight belly of Master Adamo with his fist, and Adamo hits him in the face with his arm. Nearby lies Potiphar's wife, smoking with fever. Dante watches, absorbed and fascinated, while Virgil puts a hand on his shoulder and rebukes him; Dante turns away, ashamed. The light is a faint glow from the steaming bodies, lighting the fighters from below. We see the scene at eye level, close to the fight.
```

## Canto XXXI

### inferno/31/scena-1 — Il corno e le torri

```
At the edge of the great central well, in the half-light of dusk, a thick mist hides the far side. Through the mist, tall dark shapes rise in a ring, like the towers of a castle: the giants, standing in the pit up to their navels. Dante, frightened, holds tightly to Virgil's hand and peers into the fog. From the distance, the sound of a horn, louder than the horn of Roland at Roncesvalles. The light is a faint, diffused glow in the fog, lighting the pale shapes of the giants' torsos. We see the scene from behind the poets, at eye level.
```

## Canto XXXII

### inferno/32/scena-1 — L'invocazione e il lago di ghiaccio

```
A vast, perfectly flat lake of ice stretches to the horizon, thicker than the Danube or the Don in winter. In the foreground Dante hesitates at the edge, one foot raised, because under his feet, sunk in the ice up to the face, are the heads of the traitors, livid with cold, their teeth chattering like storks' beaks. A voice has warned him not to step on them. Virgil stands behind him. The light is a cold, pale glow from the ice itself, lighting the frozen faces from below. We see the scene from a high angle, looking down at the heads.
```

## Canto XXXIII

### inferno/33/scena-1 — Ugolino comincia a parlare

```
In the frozen lake, Count Ugolino lifts his mouth from the fierce meal, his lips smeared with blood, and wipes them on the hair of the skull he is gnawing. The archbishop Ruggieri's head emerges from the ice with the back of the skull torn. Dante, standing at the edge, bends toward Ugolino, listening. Virgil stands behind him. The light is a cold, pale glow reflected from the ice, lighting Ugolino's face, with the rest in shadow. We see the scene from a slightly high angle, close to the two heads.
```

### inferno/33/scena-2 — Il sogno nella torre della fame

```
At the bottom of the image, the inside of a stone tower prison: Ugolino, bearded, lies awake beside his four young sons, who sleep huddled together. A narrow slit window lets in a thin beam of moonlight. In the upper half, his dream: a hunt on a mountain slope, where lean, eager hounds chase a wolf and its cubs, led by the archbishop Ruggieri on horseback with his men, and the dogs tear at the animals' flanks. The light is the moonlight through the slit, lighting the sleepers' faces and the hunting scene above in a pale glow. We see the scene from the side, with the tower in cross-section.
```

### inferno/33/scena-4 — L'invettiva contro Pisa

```
Dante, with one arm pointing at the sea, curses the city of Pisa. In the distance, the islands of Capraia and Gorgona are moving across the water to shut the mouth of the river Arno, and the river floods backward, drowning the city, its towers half under the water and its people swept away. In the foreground, Ugolino gnaws the skull again beside Dante. The light comes from the stormy sky, which is the brightest part of the image, lighting the flooded city. We see the scene from slightly above, with the flood in the background.
```

### inferno/33/scena-5 — La Tolomea e frate Alberigo

```
In the second ring of the ice lake, the traitors lie flat on their backs with their faces turned up, and their tears freeze in their eye sockets so that they wear crystal visors. A bitter wind blows over the ice. Friar Alberigo, his eyes covered by the frozen tears, speaks, while Dante bends over him, hesitant to break the ice. Virgil stands behind him. The light is a cold, pale glow from the ice, lighting the frozen faces and the glittering crystal over their eyes. We see the scene from a high angle, looking down at the ice.
```

## Canto XXXIV

### inferno/34/scena-1 — La Giudecca

```
In the heavy mist of dusk, a huge dark shape in the distance turns slowly like a windmill in fog, its wings beating the air: Lucifer. Dante shelters behind Virgil, hiding from the cold wind. In the foreground, the sheet of ice is full of whole human figures frozen within it, like straws in glass: some lying flat, some upright, some head down, others bent like a bow. The light is a faint, hazy glow behind the mist, silhouetting the great shape in the distance. We see the scene at eye level, from the ice.
```

### inferno/34/scena-3 — Il passaggio per il centro della Terra

```
Virgil climbs down the shaggy flank of Lucifer, between the thick matted hair and the crust of ice, with Dante clinging to his neck. Around them, the colossal bat-like wings of the monster spread into the dark. At the level of Lucifer's hips, Virgil turns, upside down, straining, to begin climbing up. The scale is gigantic, the poets tiny against the hair. The light is a faint glow coming from the ice behind, lighting the strands of hair and the poets' outlines. We see the scene from the side, close to the body of Lucifer, slightly tilted to give a sense of the turn.
```

### inferno/34/scena-4 — La natural burella

```
Dante and Virgil stand in a dark natural cave with a rough, uneven floor. Virgil points up toward a round opening in the roof, through which one sees Lucifer's two huge legs sticking up, upside down. Along the cave, a small stream winds down from the rock, and the passage climbs gently toward a distant, faint gleam of light at the far end. The light is the dim glow from the far end of the cave, which is the brightest part of the image, lighting the floor and the stream. We see the scene from within the cave, looking along the passage.
```
