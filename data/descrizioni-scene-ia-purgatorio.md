# Descrizioni delle scene del Purgatorio per il prompt Gemini

Una descrizione in inglese per ogni scena del Purgatorio senza tavola di Doré (elenco in `scene-senza-tavola-purgatorio.md`). Ognuna va incollata al posto di `[descrizione della scena…]` nel prompt di `illustrazioni-ia.md`. Ogni testo dice chi c'è, dove, cosa succede, da dove viene la luce e da che punto si guarda la scena. Nomi dei file: `img/ia/purgatorio-<NN>-scena-<K>.jpg`.

Aggiornato fino al canto XXXIII (completo).

---

## Canto I

### purgatorio/1/scena-1 — Il proemio e il cielo d'oriente

```
At dawn on a deserted shore at the foot of an immense mountain, Dante, just out of the dark underworld, lifts his face to a vast sky. Virgil stands beside him, calm, one hand lightly on Dante's shoulder. The eastern horizon glows with a pale band of light, and in it the planet Venus shines as a bright star, with a faint scatter of small clouds. High in the opposite half of the sky, four large, brilliant stars shine above the mountain, much brighter than any ordinary star. The calm sea lies flat behind them. The light comes from the four stars and from the dawn horizon, which are the brightest parts of the image; the two poets are small dark figures against the vast sky. We see the scene from behind the two poets, at ground level, looking up at the stars.
```

### purgatorio/1/scena-4 — La risposta di Catone

```
On the empty shore at dawn, the old Cato, a tall figure in a long plain robe with a long white beard falling on his chest, stands with one hand raised in a firm gesture, answering Virgil. Virgil stands before him with his head slightly bowed, respectful, and Dante kneels a little behind, hands joined, eyes lowered. Behind them the low shore reaches out to a flat sea; in the foreground small reeds grow at the water's edge. Four bright stars above Cato's head light his white beard and robe from above; the first pink glow of dawn touches the horizon. The brightest area is Cato's face and beard. We see the scene from the side, slightly below Cato, so that he towers over the two poets.
```

## Canto II

### purgatorio/2/scena-3 — La turba chiede la via

```
On a wide sunlit beach under a very bright morning sky, a crowd of newly arrived souls, just landed, stand together looking at Dante and Virgil with bewildered, uncertain faces, like travelers in a strange country. Some point towards the mountain that rises in the background, asking the way. Virgil, calm, opens his hands in a gesture that says he does not know the way either; Dante stands beside him, the souls turning to stare at him in astonishment. The sun, low on the left, rakes across the beach and lights the pale faces and shoulders of the souls. In the far distance the calm sea, and the empty sky with the tiny white sail of the departing boat. We see the scene at eye level from slightly behind the poets, with the crowd facing us.
```

### purgatorio/2/scena-4 — L'abbraccio di Casella

```
On the beach at morning, a soul steps forward from the crowd with open arms to embrace Dante. Dante has reached out to hug him, but his arms pass through the transparent figure and close on his own chest: the soul is pale and almost transparent, with a gentle smile. Casella, a slender man in a long simple robe, bends forward warmly. Virgil stands watching a little aside, calm. Behind them the crowd of other souls watches and the sea stretches away to a pale horizon. The light comes from the low morning sun on the left, falling on the hands of Dante and on the figure of Casella, which is the brightest part of the image. The viewpoint is close and frontal, centred on the failed embrace.
```

### purgatorio/2/scena-5 — Il canto di Casella e il rimprovero di Catone

```
On the shore, Casella stands singing, his face lifted and his hand on his chest, and around him Dante, Virgil and the crowd of souls listen enchanted, motionless, faces lifted, forgetting everything else. From the right, at the top of a rocky slope, Cato appears with his long white beard, arm outstretched, shouting a stern reproach. In the foreground, the first souls have already turned and run towards the mountain like startled doves leaving their food, robes streaming. The light comes from the morning sun behind Cato, making his white beard and raised arm the brightest part of the image. The viewpoint is wide and slightly high, looking across the crowd towards Cato.
```

## Canto III

### purgatorio/3/scena-1 — La ragione ha un limite

```
On an open barren plain at the foot of a great mountain, Virgil stands facing Dante and speaks to him gravely, one hand raised, his head slightly bowed as if troubled, while Dante looks at him with trust. The low sun behind Dante casts a long dark shadow of Dante's body on the ground, while Virgil casts no shadow at all, which Dante has just noticed. Far behind them rise the mountain slope and the empty sky. In the dust at their feet, faint outlines of Aristotle and Plato appear as thin, sad shadows, bowing their heads. The light comes from the low sun at the back, so the long shadow of Dante is the strongest element of the image. We see the scene from the side, at eye level, so both figures and the shadow are visible.
```

### purgatorio/3/scena-3 — Le anime come pecorelle

```
At the base of a tall rocky cliff, a flock of souls comes forward cautiously, in a line, like sheep leaving their pen: the first few step out timidly with lowered eyes, the others behind them press close together, simple and silent. The leading souls stop abruptly, seeing the shadow of Dante cast on the ground between them and the cliff, and step back. Dante and Virgil stand in the foreground at the left, seen from behind, Virgil gesturing to explain that the living body casts the shadow. The light comes from the sun low on the right, throwing the long shadow of Dante across the ground toward the souls, whose pale robes catch the light. The viewpoint is wide and slightly high, to show the entire procession.
```

### purgatorio/3/scena-4 — Manfredi

```
In the foreground, Manfredi, a young man with fair hair and a noble, gentle face, smiles sadly at Dante and opens his robe to show a deep wound across his chest; a scar cuts through one eyebrow. Dante, kneeling slightly or bending toward him, looks at the wound; Virgil stands behind them, calm. Around them a crowd of souls stands close against the great rock wall, heads lowered. In the far background above the dark cliff a pale sky: far below, a river valley with a stone bridge, where a faint cairn of stones and rain stands for his unburied body. The light comes from above left, falling on Manfredi's face and chest wound, which are the brightest parts of the image. The viewpoint is close and at eye level.
```

## Canto IV

### purgatorio/4/scena-1 — L'anima assorta nell'ascolto

```
Dante stands motionless on a rocky ledge, his head lifted and his eyes fixed, entirely absorbed in listening to the souls who are speaking with him, unaware of everything around him; in the sky behind him the sun has climbed far above the horizon, its path marked by a few thin clouds. Virgil, at his side, waits patiently with a faint smile. In the middle ground the souls of the excommunicated look up at the two poets and point toward a narrow cleft in the rock, calling out. The light comes from the climbing sun on the right, striking Dante's face and robe, which are the brightest part of the image. We see the scene from slightly below, looking up the slope toward the poets, with the cleft at right.
```

### purgatorio/4/scena-3 — La balza e il corso del sole

```
On a broad rocky ledge halfway up the mountain, Dante and Virgil sit side by side, turned toward the east, looking down the slope to the sea and the plain below. Dante has raised his eyes, astonished, to the sun, which strikes them from the left, where it should not be; Virgil, calm, explains with one hand, pointing to the sun's path across the sky, as if tracing the path of the Zodiac and the Equator. The great circle of the sky is visible, with sparse clouds, the sun low and large on the left. The light comes from the sun at the left, so the sun and the two faces turned toward it are the brightest part. We see the scene from behind and slightly above the two poets, looking out toward the distant sea.
```

## Canto V

### purgatorio/5/scena-1 — L'ombra di Dante e le voci dei pigri

```
On a steep rocky path, Dante has stopped and turned back, glancing over his shoulder at a group of the lazy souls, who have risen behind him and point at him, amazed, because the sun does not pass through his body and he casts a shadow. Virgil, a few steps ahead on the path, turns and speaks to Dante firmly, one hand raised, urging him to keep walking and ignore the gossip, like a tower unmoved by wind. The sun, low on the left, casts the long shadow of Dante on the rocks and over the faces of the souls. The light is strongest on Dante and on Virgil's raised hand. We see the scene from slightly below, looking up the path toward the two poets.
```

### purgatorio/5/scena-3 — Iacopo del Cassero

```
In a dim marsh at the foot of the mountain, reeds and shallow water in the foreground, a pale soul with a bleeding body stands among a crowd of the violently killed, speaking with one hand pointing toward the distant sea. Behind him, in a vision above the reeds, a faint scene: a man in flight through a marsh, stumbling and entangled in canes, killed by assassins, his blood spreading in the water like a small lake. Dante stands in the foreground, listening, one hand raised to his chest; Virgil waits beside him. The light comes from a pale evening sky at the left, falling on the soul's pale face and on the red-stained water, the brightest parts of the image. We see the scene from slightly above, looking along the marsh edge.
```

## Canto VI

### purgatorio/6/scena-1 — La calca delle anime e la preghiera dei vivi

```
On a steep rocky ledge at the foot of the mountain, Dante is surrounded and pressed by a crowd of pale souls who have died violent deaths, reaching toward him with outstretched hands, pleading to be remembered in the prayers of the living, like a winner at a gambling game besieged by the crowd. Dante, with a gentle gesture, promises and slowly makes his way through them; Virgil waits a few steps ahead, calm. In the front rank a few souls are drawn with distinct, anguished faces: one with a wounded throat, one with water streaming from his robe, one with hands joined in prayer. The light comes from the low evening sun on the left, striking Dante and the nearest faces, which are the brightest part of the image. The viewpoint is at eye level, slightly low, so the crowd seems to press toward us.
```

### purgatorio/6/scena-2 — Sordello abbraccia Virgilio

```
On a rocky slope above the plain at dusk, a lone soul, Sordello, proud and silent like a lion at rest, sits watching two travelers approach. As Virgil says the word "Mantua", Sordello rises and opens his arms, and the two men from the same city embrace warmly. Dante stands a little behind Virgil, hands joined, moved. In the distance the mountain slope disappears into evening mist and a few first stars appear in the sky. The light comes from the last low glow of the sun behind the mountain, a rim of brightness around the two embracing figures, which are the brightest part of the image. We see the scene from slightly below and in front, so that the embrace is silhouetted against the pale sky.
```

### purgatorio/6/scena-3 — L'invettiva contro l'Italia

```
A vast allegorical vision seen above a rocky ledge: Italy as a woman in torn robes, chained, sits slumped in the helm of a ship that drifts without a helmsman on a stormy sea, waves breaking on the rocks. In the background, a mounted saddle with no rider stands empty on the back of a wild, rearing, unbridled horse. Below, in a landscape of burning towers and fortified walls of cities, armed men of rival factions fight in the streets. At the front Dante stands small on the ledge, one arm raised in anger and grief, his cloak blown by the wind; Virgil and Sordello look on. A cold light breaks from a rent in dark storm clouds above, illuminating the weeping woman and the empty saddle, which are the brightest parts of the image. The viewpoint is wide and slightly low.
```

### purgatorio/6/scena-4 — Firenze

```
A feverish sick woman, a personification of Florence, lies tossing and turning in a great bed in a vast empty room, trying vainly to find rest among the pillows, her gaunt face contorted with pain. Around her bed, on the walls and the floor, hang and lie scattered symbols of constant change: scrolls of laws, coins, banners and robes of office, thrown off and replaced. Through a high window behind, the towers of a medieval city are visible under a pale sky; at the window, three small figures of Dante, Virgil and Sordello look in, grave and ironical. The light comes from the window at the back and falls on the bed and on the sick woman's hand, which is the brightest part of the image. We see the scene from a slightly high angle.
```

## Canto VII

### purgatorio/7/scena-2 — Il Limbo e la legge della notte

```
At sunset on a rocky mountain ledge, Sordello kneels and draws a line in the dust with his finger, showing Virgil and Dante the limit they may not cross after the sun has gone down. Virgil, standing beside him and listening gravely, gestures toward the line; Dante, a little behind, looks toward the western horizon where the last red sun rests on the sea. In the background, a faint vision above the horizon of the pale shadows of Limbo: children and noble pagans standing in a gloomy, twilit place, sighing. The light comes from the low sun at the back, throwing long shadows across the rocks and the drawn line, which is the brightest area of the foreground. The viewpoint is low, looking along the line toward the sunset.
```

### purgatorio/7/scena-4 — I principi negligenti

```
From a grassy ledge above a small flowering valley at dusk, Sordello, Dante and Virgil look down upon a gathering of crowned princes seated on the grass in the valley, among flowers: an emperor at the highest seat, silent, with a vacant, regretful look; a king beside him comforting him; two others in close conversation, one beating his breast and the other resting his cheek on his hand; a strong, bearded king singing beside a big-nosed one, a youth behind them; a simple king sitting apart; and a marquis seated lowest on the ground, looking up. All wear crowns and rich robes, their faces sad and noble, singing a hymn. The last light of the setting sun comes from the left and lights the crowns and robes of the princes, which are the brightest area of the image. The viewpoint is from above and slightly behind the three poets.
```

## Canto VIII

### purgatorio/8/scena-2 — Nino Visconti

```
In a grassy valley at twilight, Dante and Judge Nino Visconti greet each other with open arms, the soul's face glad to see Dante among the saved. Nino is a noble, gentle man in a long robe. Behind them, Sordello and Virgil watch; to one side, another soul, Corrado, sits among the souls of the valley and turns toward the group, called by Nino. Around them the souls of princes in the valley stand in small groups among flowers, their faces dim in the dusk. The light comes from a pale evening sky at the left, falling on the two embracing faces, which are the brightest area of the image. We see the scene at eye level from slightly to the side.
```

### purgatorio/8/scena-4 — Corrado Malaspina

```
In a flowery valley at night, Corrado Malaspina, a mature nobleman in a rich robe, stands facing Dante with one hand open as if listening, while Dante, with a hand on his chest, praises the fame of his house. Virgil and Sordello stand to one side, silent. Behind Corrado in a vision above the hills a faint image of a mountain valley with castles, and a sun setting seven times in the sign of the Ram, shown as seven small suns on an arc above a ram's horns. The light comes from the first stars in the dark sky and from a pale glow on the horizon, falling on Corrado's face, which is the brightest part of the image. The viewpoint is close and at eye level.
```

## Canto IX

### purgatorio/9/scena-2 — Lucia ha portato Dante

```
On a mountain ledge at early morning, with a bright sun already high over a calm sea behind them, Virgil stands facing Dante, who has just woken and looks around, bewildered and anxious; Virgil points up to the high rock wall above them, showing the opening in it that marks the entrance to Purgatory. In the sky above, in a bright vision between the clouds, Saint Lucy, a young woman with a lamp, lifts the sleeping Dante in her arms and carries him up the mountain, her robe flowing. The light comes from the rising sun at the back and from the vision of Lucy, which is the brightest part of the image. We see the scene from slightly below, looking up toward the rock wall and the vision.
```

## Canto X

### purgatorio/10/scena-1 — La pietra fessa e la prima cornice

```
Dante and Virgil climb up through a narrow, winding cleft in a vast rock, the walls rising dark and smooth on both sides, curving like a wave moving to and fro, so that they have to lean first to one side and then to the other. Behind them, far below, the great gate is closing and its leaves swing shut. Ahead of them at the top of the cleft opens a bright empty ledge, a plain deserted like a desert track, with a high wall of white marble rising behind it. A thin crescent moon sets in the sky above. The light comes from the opening at the top, from the pale sky and the white marble of the ledge, which is the brightest part of the image. The viewpoint is from below, looking up the cleft toward the ledge.
```

### purgatorio/10/scena-2 — L'Annunciazione scolpita

```
On a ledge of the mountain, a high wall of dazzling white marble carved in deep, lifelike bas-relief with the Annunciation: the angel Gabriele, with great wings, in a gentle gesture, speaks "Ave" to Mary, who kneels with hands joined and bowed head, the words "Behold the handmaid of the Lord" almost visible in her pose. The carving is so alive that the figures seem to breathe. Dante and Virgil stand small in the foreground, seen from behind, looking up at the relief with wonder, Virgil gesturing for Dante to look further along the wall, where another carved scene begins. The light comes from the left, from the pale sky, raking across the relief and making the white marble the brightest part of the image. The viewpoint is low, looking up at the relief from the two poets' position.
```

### purgatorio/10/scena-4 — I superbi sotto i massi

```
On the narrow marble ledge, a line of the proud souls advances slowly toward Dante and Virgil, each bent double or crouching under an enormous boulder on his back: some are almost entirely folded, knees pressed to chest like the stone corbels that support a ceiling, faces contorted, some beating their breasts, one weeping and seeming to say "I can bear no more". The heaviest burdens bend the figures lowest. Dante and Virgil stand in the foreground at the left, Virgil gesturing for Dante to look closely under the stones. The ledge winds around the mountain into the distance, with the white marble wall at the left and an abyss at the right. The light comes from the pale sky above the abyss and falls on the bowed, naked backs and the boulders, which are the brightest part of the image. We see the scene from slightly above, along the ledge.
```

## Canto XI

### purgatorio/11/scena-1 — Il «Padre nostro» dei superbi

```
On a narrow ledge of the mountain, a line of the proud souls walks slowly, each bent double under an enormous boulder on his back, faces towards the ground, hands joined or pressed against their chests, praying aloud. Their lips move in prayer and a few lift their eyes toward the sky with great effort. In the foreground at the left, Dante and Virgil stand listening, Dante with head bowed, Virgil calm. On the high wall at the right, faint carved letters hint at the words of the prayer. Above, a break in the clouds, and rays of light that fall on the line of the bent souls, which is the brightest part of the image. The viewpoint is from the side, slightly high, along the ledge.
```

### purgatorio/11/scena-2 — Omberto Aldobrandesco

```
On the stony ledge, Omberto Aldobrandesco, a heavy-set nobleman, crouches nearly folded in two under an immense stone, the boulder crushing his shoulders. He turns his face sideways and upward with great effort to speak to Dante, who bends toward him, his face level with Omberto's. Virgil stands beside Dante, calm and silent. Behind Omberto in the shadow, other souls bent under stones, and above them a faint carved scene on the rock wall of a castle on a hill. The light comes from the left and strikes Omberto's face and the great stone, which are the brightest parts of the image. We see the scene close, at eye level, with Dante in the foreground.
```

### purgatorio/11/scena-3 — Oderisi da Gubbio

```
Oderisi da Gubbio, a thin middle-aged miniature painter in a simple robe, crouches under a boulder and looks up toward Dante, who has bent his head to talk to him. In the background, a hazy vision on a wall of drifting painted pages of illuminated manuscripts, with tiny figures, and above them the figures of Cimabue and Giotto, one fading, the other bright. At the right, Virgil watches. The light comes from the left, striking the face of Oderisi and the pages in the vision, which are the brightest part of the image. The viewpoint is close and level with the two faces.
```

### purgatorio/11/scena-4 — Provenzan Salvani

```
At the rim of a tall wall of rock, a vision hovers over the ledge: in the great square of Siena, Provenzan Salvani, a proud nobleman, stands trembling in the middle of the crowd, hand extended, begging for coins for the ransom of a friend, his proud posture broken. In the foreground, Oderisi bends under his boulder, pointing toward the vision; Dante bends to listen, and Virgil stands calm beside him. The light comes from above in a shaft of pale sunlight over the square in the vision, and the begging figure of Provenzano is the brightest part of the image. The viewpoint is slightly low, looking up at the vision.
```

## Canto XII

### purgatorio/12/scena-1 — Il pavimento scolpito

```
Dante and Virgil walk side by side on the ledge, Dante with eyes lowered toward the pavement, which is carved all over with detailed figures like a great tomb slab. In the foreground, at their feet, large engraved scenes begin: a falling angel in flames, a fallen giant, tiny armies. Behind them, a line of the proud souls crouches under boulders. Virgil points down at the pavement and gestures for Dante to look, while Dante bends slightly, one hand at his chest. The light comes from the left, raking across the carved stone and making the carvings stand out, so the engraved figures are the brightest part of the image. We see the scene from above and slightly behind the two poets, looking down at the pavement.
```

### purgatorio/12/scena-4 — L'angelo dell'umiltà

```
On a rocky ledge at midday, the angel of humility, a young figure in a white robe with great wings partly spread and arms open in welcome, descends toward Dante and Virgil, his face shining like the morning star, the rays of light streaming from his face and hair. Virgil points up with one hand and with the other makes a gesture of reverence, urging Dante to look up; Dante straightens, shielding his eyes. Behind the angel the high rock wall shows a cut staircase leading up. The angel is the strongest light source and the brightest part of the image; the two poets are dark figures at the lower left. We see the scene from slightly below.
```

### purgatorio/12/scena-5 — Il primo P cancellato

```
On a steep staircase cut into the rock, Dante climbs behind Virgil, his head lifted, lighter in his step, while the angel stands at the top of the stairs and touches Dante's brow with one wing, brushing away a letter P from his forehead. Seven letter Ps had been traced there; six remain, in faint small marks. Around them the high walls rise on both sides. Virgil turns half toward Dante and smiles. The light comes from the angel at the top and falls on Dante's forehead and the wing, which are the brightest part of the image. We see the scene from below, looking up the stairs toward the angel.
```

## Canto XIII

### purgatorio/13/scena-1 — La seconda cornice e le voci della carità

```
On a bare ledge with a smooth livid wall of stone, without any figure or relief, Virgil stands with arms raised toward the sun, turning his body to the right, his face lifted toward it as to a guide, and Dante, small beside him, looks at him. In the empty air above the ledge, faint half-transparent spirits in flight, winged, with long robes, pass crying, as if throwing words: one with a cup, one with a hand on his chest, one with outstretched arms. The light comes from the sun in the sky at the upper left, falling on Virgil's face and raised hands, which are the brightest part of the image. The viewpoint is low, looking up at the sky.
```

### purgatorio/13/scena-3 — Sapia

```
In the middle of the line of seated envious souls, all in coarse cloaks the colour of the rock, their eyelids sewn with iron wire, a woman, Sapia, lifts her chin like a blind person, turning her head toward Dante's voice. Her mouth is open, speaking; tears run down from under her sewn eyelids. Dante bends toward her, one hand on his chest, and Virgil stands behind him. Around her the other souls lean on one another, their shoulders touching, supported by the rock wall behind. The light comes from the left and illuminates Sapia's raised face and her sewn eyes, the brightest part of the image. The viewpoint is close, at eye level with Sapia.
```

## Canto XIV

### purgatorio/14/scena-1 — Guido del Duca e Rinieri: la valle dell'Arno

```
Two envious souls, seated side by side against the rock wall, lean toward each other and then lift their faces upward, sewn eyelids closed, listening to Dante's voice; one of them speaks, one hand raised. Dante stands in front of them, bending slightly, one arm pointing in the direction of the Arno valley. In the background, a vision in the air above the ledge: the course of a river from a mountain through a valley, along which crowds of pigs, then snarling dogs, then wolves, then foxes appear one after another, drawn as small human-animal figures. The light comes from the upper left, falling on the two raised faces, which are the brightest part of the image. The viewpoint is at eye level, a little to the side.
```

### purgatorio/14/scena-2 — Guido del Duca e la Romagna

```
Guido del Duca, a gaunt man with sewn eyelids, leans forward, one hand outstretched toward the horizon, mourning the lost knights and ladies of Romagna, while Rinieri sits silent next to him. In the air behind them, a faint procession of vanished figures: knights in armour, ladies in long gowns, fortified towns on distant hills, all fading into mist. Dante stands before them, bowing his head, with one hand against his chest. The light comes from a pale sky at the left and strikes Guido's face and hand, which are the brightest part of the image. The viewpoint is at eye level.
```

### purgatorio/14/scena-3 — Le voci dell'invidia punita

```
On a dark ledge, Dante has stopped and stepped back, pressing himself against Virgil in fear, as a thunderclap and a flash of lightning cross the sky: in the stormy air a vision of Cain with a wild, desperate face and raised arms running, fleeing from the sound of his own cry; in a second flash, behind, a vision of Aglauro turning into stone, her body half transformed into a grey statue, a hand extended. Virgil stands calm, looking upward, one arm raised toward the sky as if explaining. The light comes from the lightning in the sky above, striking the figures of Cain and Aglauro, which are the brightest part of the image. The viewpoint is from slightly below, looking up at the visions.
```

## Canto XV

### purgatorio/15/scena-1 — La luce dell'angelo

```
On a ledge with the low sun in the west, Dante lifts one hand over his eyebrows to shield his eyes from a dazzling light reflected in front of him, as if from a mirror, while Virgil beside him gestures calmly toward the source: far ahead, still small, a radiant figure of an angel coming toward them on the ledge, its brightness spreading as if reflecting from water, producing a long streak of light that cuts the dark rock. The light comes from the angel and from the low sun behind it, and the angel is the brightest part of the image; the two poets are dark figures in the foreground. We see the scene from behind them, at eye level.
```

### purgatorio/15/scena-2 — L'angelo della misericordia e il bene condiviso

```
At the foot of a rock staircase, a radiant angel in a white robe, with great wings half open, stands inviting Dante and Virgil with an open hand to climb. Virgil stands beside Dante and, gesturing with one hand as he speaks, explains the nature of love; in the sky behind him, a vision of a great circle of souls in heaven, a many-pointed star of shared light, each soul reflecting the light of the others like a mirror. Dante listens, looking up. The light comes from the angel and from the vision above, and the angel is the brightest part of the image. The viewpoint is from below, looking up the staircase toward the angel.
```

### purgatorio/15/scena-4 — Il fumo

```
On a ledge at sunset, Dante is staggering, half-woken from a trance, his eyes veiled and his legs crossed beneath him as if drunk, while Virgil holds him by the arm, speaking to him. Behind them the low sun is setting, its last rays raking over the rock. In front of them, a dense black smoke, dark as night, rolls over the ledge toward the two poets, filling the path and the air, from a wall of cloud rising from the abyss at the right. The light comes from the setting sun behind Dante, which is the brightest part of the image, while the smoke swallows everything ahead into darkness. We see the scene from slightly behind and at eye level, looking toward the advancing smoke.
```

## Canto XVI

### purgatorio/16/scena-1 — Il fumo e l'«Agnus Dei»

```
Inside a vast, dense black cloud of smoke that fills a narrow ledge of the mountain, Dante walks with eyes shut and one hand on Virgil's shoulder, like a blind man following his guide, nothing visible around them but swirling, heavy smoke, a darker night than any night. Virgil leads, looking ahead calmly, one hand reaching back to keep Dante close. Faintly in the smoke around them, barely visible voices as pale silhouettes of kneeling souls with raised hands and open mouths, singing together. The only light is a pale, weak glow coming from one spot of the smoke at the upper left, falling on Virgil's face and shoulder, which are the brightest part of the image. We see the scene from the side, at eye level, tightly framed.
```

### purgatorio/16/scena-3 — Libero arbitrio e i due soli

```

In a divine forest, the great mystical procession turns around and marches in the opposite direction: seven candelabra flames, white-robed elders, winged beasts, and a great chariot drawn by a griffin, moving as a host under shields turning together; Matelda, Statius and Dante follow the right wheel. Dante, sleepy, lies down on the grass and falls asleep as the hymn plays. The light comes from the seven flames at the front of the procession, and the blossoming tree, the brightest part of the image. The viewpoint is slightly high, wide.
```

### purgatorio/32/scena-2 — Beatrice sotto l'albero

```
Dante, lying on the grass beneath the blooming tree, is wakened by a splendour and a voice, and looks up in awe, as the apostles Peter, John and James who, after the Transfiguration, wake from sleep and see Moses and Elijah vanished and their master changed; above him leans Matelda, pointing toward the tree. Under the tree, Beatrice sits alone on the bare earth as a guard left with the chariot, in a circle formed by seven nymphs holding burning lamps shielded from wind. In the sky, a vision of the heavenly Rome, with towers and domes, and the figure of Christ. The light comes from the seven lamps around Beatrice, the brightest part of the image. The viewpoint is slightly low, from Dante's lying position.
```

### purgatorio/32/scena-3 — L'aquila, la volpe e il drago

```
Under the blooming tree in the forest, a vast eagle plunges down from the sky like lightning through the branches, tearing bark and flowers as it dives, striking the chariot so hard that it rocks like a ship in a storm. In the next stage, a gaunt, hungry fox slinks into the chariot's cradle, and Beatrice, standing beside, rebukes her and drives her off. Then the eagle descends again and leaves its feathers on the chariot. In the lower part of the image, a great dragon breaks out of the earth between the wheels, its tail fixed in the chariot, and drags away part of the floor like a wasp withdrawing its sting. A voice from the sky: the angels sing in mourning. The light comes from a rift in the sky above and the flash of the eagle's dive, the brightest part of the image. The viewpoint is wide and slightly high.
```

## Canto XXXIII

### purgatorio/33/scena-1 — Il lamento delle donne

```
Seven women, three in front and four behind, stand at the side of the great chariot in the divine forest, hands clasped, singing a lament and weeping, their white and coloured robes falling in folds. In front of them Beatrice, tall, sighing, stands with an expression of deep sorrow, like Mary at the foot of the cross, her face lit with a rosy glow; she raises one hand and speaks. Beside her, Matelda, Statius and Dante stand at a distance, watching, Dante bowing his head. The light comes from Beatrice's face and the glow of her robes, the brightest part of the image. The viewpoint is at eye level.
```

### purgatorio/33/scena-2 — La profezia del DXV

```
Beatrice, calm and solemn, walks ahead with Dante at her side and speaks to him gravely, one hand raised, the other open toward him; behind them in a great vision above the forest, the chariot transformed into a monster, with an enormous eagle's feathers, and an angelic messenger descending from the stars, holding a sword, striking down a giant and a veiled woman. Beneath the vision, written on a floating scroll, the number five hundred ten and five, in Roman numerals DXV, surrounded by the figures of the Sphinx and Themis, the Naiads untying riddles. Dante, bowed, listens, holding his staff wrapped with palm. The light comes from the vision of the stars and the avenging messenger, the brightest part of the image. The viewpoint is slightly low, looking up.
```
